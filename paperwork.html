From quote to capture day

Four documents to start a first project, fewer after that. Here's the order they come in.

  1. 1First

    Tell us about the site

    Send the address, rough size, and what the capture is for through the project form. We reply with questions and a project price. Nothing is booked yet.

  2. 2Once, ever

    Sign the ground rules

    The Services Agreement holds the standing terms. Sign it with your first project and every project after that uses it, so returning clients skip this step.

  3. 3Every project

    Approve the job

    The Capture Order sets the scope, date, deliverables, and price. Signing it and paying the deposit is what books the day.

  4. 4Before the day

    Confirm permission

    The Access Authorization is signed by whoever controls the property. If that's your landlord, manager, or venue rather than you, it goes to them to sign.

  5. 5The week of

    Get the space ready

    Work through the Site Prep & Privacy Checklist. It has its own page you can tick off or print, and we go over it with your site contact when we arrive.

  6. 6After

    Sign off the delivery

    When the scene is delivered, a one-page Delivery & Acceptance form records what you received and closes the job.

What's in the packet

The four documents every first project needs. Each one is a few pages and does one job.

Signed once01

Spatial Capture Services Agreement

The standing terms between us. It doesn't describe any one job. It sets the rules every job runs on, so each new project only needs a short order.

Who signs
You or your organization, and us
When
With your first Capture Order

Worth knowing

  • Once paid, you get a permanent license to use your deliverables for your own work, marketing, and projects.
  • The scene is a visual record. Its measurements aren't certified for survey, engineering, or code use.
  • Raw capture files may be deleted 90 days after delivery unless you've arranged archival storage.
One per job02

Project / Capture Order

The job itself: the address, which areas are in and out of scope, the date, what you'll receive, how long it's hosted, and the price with deposit and balance terms.

Who signs
You and us
When
After you approve the quote. It books the day.

Worth knowing

  • Anything written here overrides the Agreement, for this job only.
  • No-capture rooms and areas are listed here, in writing, before we arrive.
  • Private-only hosting, offline processing, or deletion deadlines need to be in it before capture.
Permission to be there03

Property Access & Capture Authorization

Confirms the signer can let us in and allow the space to be recorded. It also lists restricted areas, known hazards, animals, alarms, and escort rules.

Who signs
Whoever controls the property: owner, tenant with authority, manager, or authorized agent
When
Before capture day

Worth knowing

  • It permits capture and processing only. Showing your property publicly needs a separate release.
  • We don't drill, fasten to, or move fixtures, finishes, or collections without asking.
  • We can pause or stop if conditions on site turn out to be unsafe.
Your to-do list04

Site Preparation & Privacy Checklist

What to put away, open up, and tell us about. The capture sees in detail, and whatever is left visible in a capture area can end up in the scene.

Who signs
You or your site contact, with our operator
When
Worked through beforehand, confirmed on arrival

Worth knowing

  • The full checklist has its own page. Tick it off there or print it.
  • If something has to stay in place but shouldn't be recorded, point it out and we'll route around it.

Only if your project needs them

Most projects never see these. Ask for any of them when you request a quote.

  • Confidential Capture / NDA Addendum

    For sites where the location, contents, or scene itself has to stay confidential. It can require private-only delivery or limit cloud processing, and it keeps confidentiality in place for three years after delivery by default.

    Security-sensitive sites, unreleased productions, private collections

  • Portfolio / Public Display Release

    A simple yes or no on whether we can show your project in our portfolio, named or anonymous. Saying no changes nothing about the job. Even a yes never lets us publish a private street address, share raw files, or use your capture to train AI.

    Anyone happy to let their space be seen, or not

  • Model Ownership & License Schedule

    For when the standard license isn't enough: public commercial use, exclusive rights, source files like PLY, meshes, or Unity and Unreal projects, or a full buyout. It lists exactly which files you receive and what you can do with them.

    Film and VFX teams, developers, game and VR projects
After capture day

Two short forms to close things out

Both are a single page, and both keep changes and handover in writing.

  • Change / Rescan AuthorizationIf plans changeAdds a room, a return visit, another deliverable, or a new date. It shows the extra fee and revised total up front, and nothing outside the original scope starts until it's signed.
  • Delivery & Acceptance FormAt handoverRecords what was delivered and where, then your call: accepted, accepted with minor fixes, or a rescan requested. It also notes the final balance and hosting term.

Straight answers

The questions people ask before they sign, answered from the documents themselves.

Who owns the capture?

You keep every right you already have in the property and what's in it. We keep the raw capture and working files. Once the project is paid, you get a permanent license to use the deliverables for your own operations, marketing, documentation, and projects. If you need source files, exclusive rights, or full ownership, the License Schedule covers it.

Will you post my property online?

Not without your written permission. Capturing a space never gives us the right to show it. Portfolio use needs a separate release, and saying no to it doesn't change anything about your project.

Where is the data processed?

Captures are processed with specialist cloud reconstruction services and stored in our normal delivery workflow. If you need restricted or offline processing, regional hosting, or a deletion deadline, put it in the Capture Order before capture. It can affect price and schedule.

How long do you keep the raw data?

Raw captures and working files may be deleted 90 days after final delivery. If you want them kept for future reprocessing, ask about an archival master.

When do I pay?

A deposit when you sign the Capture Order, as it states. Invoices are due within 15 days unless the order says otherwise. Final downloads and source files are released once the balance is paid.

What if something's wrong with the delivery?

Tell us within 7 days of delivery. If it's our processing error, we fix it, and if a return visit is really needed because of our mistake, that's included. If the site itself has changed since capture day, a new capture is added work under a Change / Rescan Authorization.

Is this a survey?

No. It's a photorealistic record for viewing, documentation, planning, and creative work. It isn't a licensed survey, inspection, or engineering measurement. If you need measured drawings or survey-grade data, tell us early and we'll point you to the right workflow.

Can I reschedule?

Rescheduling and cancellation terms for your project are written into the Capture Order, so you'll see them before you sign. Weather, site closures, and other things outside either side's control don't count against anyone.

Can you provide proof of insurance?

For commercial projects, ask and we'll provide a certificate of insurance. Additional-insured status, special endorsements, or unusually high limits need advance notice and can affect the price, so mention them with your quote request.

Can my lawyer read the documents first?

Of course. Email us and we'll send the current set, blank, for review before you request a quote.

These summaries are here to help you read the documents, not to replace them. The signed documents for your project are what count. Our agreements are governed by Missouri law.
