What is a Gaussian splat?

A photograph you can move through

Gaussian splatting turns many views of a real place into a photorealistic 3D scene. Instead of looking at a set of fixed 360° panoramas, you can move through the captured space and see it from new angles.

The capture combines imagery, camera position, and spatial data into a scene designed for realistic viewing and reuse in modern 3D workflows.

  1. 1Many views

    We walk the space and record it from lots of positions.

  2. 2Soft 3D splats

    Those views become millions of tiny, soft points of color placed in space.

  3. 3Move through it

    You open a link and look around from angles no one photographed.

The St. Louis Spatial Archive

Some places deserve more than photographs.

Historic places, preserved as places.

St. Louis is full of rooms, homes, factories, churches, theaters, and details that may never look exactly the same again. We are building an evolving spatial archive of remarkable local places, from active restorations to intact time capsules, so people can experience them as spaces rather than only as photographs.

A capture can preserve a building before work begins, document each stage of a restoration, or create a permanent interactive record of a finished space.

How a capture day goes

A capture is a slow, methodical walk through the space. Here's what to expect, from planning to the link in your inbox. How long the day takes depends on size and complexity, and we'll estimate it when we quote.

  1. 1Before the day

    Plan the visit

    We confirm the address, which areas are in scope, keys and access, parking, and who we'll meet on site. For occupied spaces we agree on any rooms that are off limits.

  2. 2The day before

    Get the space ready

    Lights on, blinds set how you want them seen, interior doors open, and anything private put away. Whatever is in the room on the day is what ends up in the scene.

  3. 3On arrival

    Walk it together

    We walk the space with you or your site contact, flag tricky spots like mirrors, glass, tight stairs, and bright windows, and plan a route through every area.

  4. 4Capture

    Room by room

    We move slowly through each area with a handheld capture rig, overlapping passes so every surface is seen from several angles. People and pets stay out of the room being captured; work can carry on elsewhere.

  5. 5Before we leave

    Check coverage

    We review what we captured while we're still on site and go back for any gaps, so a missed corner gets caught before we pack up.

  6. 6After

    Process and deliver

    The capture is reconstructed, reviewed for missing areas and visual problems, cleaned up, and delivered as a link with any agreed files. Recurring projects repeat the same route so dated captures line up.

Everything in step 1 is written down before the day: scope, access, no-capture areas, and price. See the paperwork, in plain English, and the site prep checklist you can tick off online.

Good to know

These are photorealistic spatial captures intended for viewing, documentation, presentation, preservation, and creative 3D workflows. They are not licensed land surveys, code inspections, engineering measurements, or a substitute for survey-grade point clouds or CAD documentation.

If a project requires measured drawings, engineering-grade accuracy, BIM, or certified survey work, flag that during intake so the correct workflow can be recommended.

Need an archival master?

Ask about preserving the project's source and master capture data for future reuse or reprocessing.
